# Minds: decision engines from the atoms up

**Date:** 2026-09-27
**Status:** in progress. Minds 1 is designed (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-minds-1-utility-design.md`).
**Separate from:** the reproductions (`docs/papers.md`), which keep their own milestone numbers.
This program's steps are numbered Minds 1, 2, …. Like the war program, these are our own
experiments, built on a model we have reproduced.
**Serves:** the social programs that need agents with real decision procedures. These are exchange
(Hornvale's asks), swarm coordination (`2026-09-27-swarm-coordination.md`) and, later, deception
and betrayal.

## Principle

A decision engine is an instrument. Before any social result rests on one, it has to pass two
tests:

- **Verification:** it gives the known right answer where one exists.
- **Validation:** it produces, or fails to produce, an attested behavioral regularity, with the
  failure reported as carefully as the success.

Most of the validation targets come from foraging theory, because it has what social theory mostly
lacks: quantitative, replicated predictions about where individuals go and when they leave.

## Method

- **One atom at a time.** Each engine is a milestone with its sources, its oracle, its target,
  presets, sweeps and survey claims, like the reproductions.
- **Every engine reduces to rule M.** Configured to see only what rule M sees and to value only
  what rule M values, each engine must reproduce rule M's fingerprints on every golden Sugarscape
  preset. This is the program's standing check that its engines agree with each other. Where an
  engine can't reduce (a planner whose search order draws differently), the difference is named
  and tested.
- **Literal default, named switch.** The decision rule is a switch on the Sugarscape config.
  `book` stays the default, and every golden entry stays unedited.
- **Findings are the point.** Where the book's rule, or an engine, fails a regularity, the failure
  is the result. Say which part of the regularity's assumptions it breaks.
- **Deterministic and cheap.** Seeded, replayable, native and WASM agreeing. Cost per Flump per
  tick is reported for each engine.

## The atoms

| Step | Atom | Verification | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minds 1 | The decision seam and the **utility mind** (Mark, 2009; Dill and Mark's infinite-axis utility) | Reduces to rule M on every golden preset | The **ideal free distribution** (Fretwell and Lucas, 1970) and the undermatching of field data (Kennedy and Gray, 1993) |
| Minds 2 | **A\*** and walking (Hart, Nilsson and Raphael, 1968) | Optimal lengths on grid benchmarks (Sturtevant, 2012); admissibility and consistency | Rule M's one-tick jump against walking a path: which of the book's results survive |
| Minds 3 | **Memory and belief**: sites seen, their ages, and fading | Hand-built histories; docking with Hornvale's fading beliefs | The **marginal value theorem** (Charnov, 1976); ideal-free matching beyond sight |
| Minds 4 | **GOAP** (Fikes and Nilsson, 1971; Orkin, 2006) | Plan lengths against exhaustive search on small STRIPS domains | **Central-place foraging** (Orians and Pearson, 1979): load size rises with distance |
| Minds 5 | **Behavior trees** (Isla, 2005; Colledanchise and Ögren, 2018) | The formal tick semantics; a rule-M tree reduces to rule M | The foraging targets, as the structured reactive baseline |
| Minds 6 | **HTN** (Erol, Hendler and Nau, 1994) | Plans on Minds 4's domains match GOAP's where both apply | What prewritten methods buy or cost against search |

Then:

- **Compositions:** utility picks the goal, GOAP plans it, A* prices the moves.
- **Harder scenarios:** tools, exclusive sites, carrying, seasons.
- **Scaling:** Flumps × planning horizon × world size, and where each engine stops being affordable.

## Where the social programs join

- **Exchange** (asking and answering between selfish agents) needs individuals who can tell each
  other apart (Sugarscape's ids), memory of each relation that fades (Minds 3), and the holder's
  cost of giving over a horizon (foresight welfare, already in the engine). It needs no planner,
  so it can start after Minds 3.
- **Swarm coordination** needs walking (Minds 2), memory (Minds 3) and a planner (Minds 4).
- **Deception, betrayal and scheming** need false signals, promises, reputation and models of other
  minds. It's written up only once exchange and swarm results show which of these are needed.

## What's already known

From a throwaway probe while designing Minds 1: two cone patches (input ratios 1, 2.1 and 4.4),
100 Flumps of metabolism 1, 10 seeds, the book's rule M.

- **Rule M undermatches:** *s* ≈ 0.75 at vision 1–6 and 0.82–0.92 at vision 10–20, in the
  direction of most field data.
- **"Free" fails before "ideal" does.** Rule M keeps a Flump in place when nothing it sees is
  better. A Flump that starts out of sight of sugar never moves: 55–70 of 100 at vision 1–6.
  The book's Flumps can't explore.
