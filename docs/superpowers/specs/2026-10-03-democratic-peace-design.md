## Objective and boundary

Make tagging, defensive alliances and collective security inspectable, and compare democratic survival, territorial expansion and clustering with the paper's experiments. Success is an auditable reproduction finding, including failure, unavailable comparison or dependence on a reconstruction choice. Preventing democratic–democratic attack is partly stipulated and cannot itself establish a substantive coordination result.

The primary preset preserves the printed decreasing attack/victory probability. Its incompatibility with superiority prose is visible and testable. An increasing alternative is separately named and never silently substituted after observing results. No 2003 damage, technology, alert, campaign, war-size or shadow collector is present. Cederman/Rao empirical dyad-year inference and Cederman/Gleditsch 2004 contextual regime change remain future work with their own sources and statistics. No historical fit, causal democratic-peace conclusion, learned cooperation or AI-intent inference follows from these lattice histories.

## Architecture and ownership

Add `ModelKind::DemocraticPeace`, `ModelConfig::DemocraticPeace(DemocraticPeaceConfig)` and `DemocraticPeaceWorld` in `crates/sugarscape-core/src/democratic_peace/`. Use the existing model dispatch, schema, seeded portable RNG, snapshot, rendering, outcome and native/WASM hosts. Independently implement the world pipeline and science census. Separate modules handle config/presets, territory, resources, decisions, alliances, combat/claims, statistics and views. Keep responsibilities small; do not introduce an inheritance hierarchy or a generic geopolitical engine.

Reuse only neutral helpers whose explicit inputs cover the required behavior: cardinal adjacency, stable identity concepts, contiguous reachability and conditional-commitment arithmetic. Initially copying independently derived small formulas is preferable to changing GeoSim's public bindings or introducing a source-specific dependency. Any extracted neutral helper must preserve existing GeoSim behavior and evidence identity; the completed study's source/binary inventories and verdicts are never rewritten. Recovered third-party model code remains reference-only.

Cell IDs are fixed row-major integers. Sovereign IDs are `(capital_cell, sovereignty_generation)`; release increments the cell's checked generation counter. Primitive cells never disappear. Cells store owner, immutable initial regime and mutable latent regime tag. States store ID, current governing regime, resources and sorted member cells. Regime is the sovereign capital's current tag. Front keys are sorted pairs of sovereign IDs and retain directional previous actions, old/current commitments, current actions, previous initiation flags, battle path, claims, and probabilities. Fronts link sovereign territorial neighbors; multiple cell-border edges form one front. Retired state IDs remain in structural records.

Alliance identity is `(threat_id, creation_period, serial)`. Its members and threat are sovereign IDs; adjacency to the threat is required for membership. An actor has at most one defensive alliance. Global collective security is a separate obligation mechanism, not a second defensive-alliance membership. An alliance's pooled deterrent resources sum members' individual directional commitments toward its named threat; it does not create resources or directly replace the combat balance. The pariah set and obligation reasons are recorded per period. Ordered maps/sets and row-major traversal make reductions explicit.

## Resolved readings and alternatives

All alternatives below have exact behavior; none is claimed as the original author algorithm. Primary defaults are in bold. UI and export report both value and source status.

| Zero ratio |**`equal_zero_neutral`**: zero/zero probability .5 and threat ratio 1; positive/zero uses the direction's analytic limit; zero/positive uses its zero limit|`reject_zero_denominator`: any probability/threat calculation with denominator0 invalidates the attempted period; zero numerator with positive denominator uses the formula limit|

| Enemy total |**`active_fronts`**: sum old incoming commitments where either previous action isD|`all_fronts`: sum old incoming commitments on every eligible external front|
| Inactive commitment formula |**`per_front_opponent`**: each inactive denominator adds its own old opposing commitment|`first_inactive_opponent`: repeat the old opposing commitment of the lowest-ID inactive front in every inactive denominator, reflecting the anomalous repeated term in p.495's example; invalid positive/zero denominator is explicit|
| Latent regime inheritance |**`persistent_cell_tags`**: occupation changes governing regime only, leaving each cell's latent tag; released cells govern using that tag|`overwrite_on_conquest`: every absorbed cell's latent tag becomes winner regime; released cells use the resulting tag|

| Opposing victories |**`independent_claims`**: independent tests for both sides; record both successes and let structural order/locks resolve|`single_draw`: one draw assigns i victory on[0,qi), j on[qi,qi+qj) after dividing qi,qj by max(1,qi+qj); remaining interval is undecided|

The repeated-opponent alternative above is a precise reconstruction of an erroneous worked-example pattern, not a claim that the paper intended that algorithm. All alternatives remain available for exploratory inspection; the initial registered study below measures the primary bundle. It makes no robustness claim over the unmeasured alternatives. A prospectively approved separate study is required before alternative results receive registered scientific verdicts.

Fixed allocation is `(1-mobile_share)*resources/n`; the mobile pool is `mobile_share*resources`. The fixed-allocation denominator `n` is the count of eligible distinct neighboring sovereign-state fronts, not border cells or edge length. For a predator all sovereign neighbors are eligible; for a democracy only nondemocratic neighbors are eligible. When `n=0`, all commitments are 0 and no division is evaluated; the state's capacity remains available but unused. Ineligible democratic–democratic commitments are 0. `enemy_total` excludes these ineligible fronts under both readings. If enemy total0, each eligible commitment is fixed+whole mobile pool; otherwise active commitment is fixed+mobile×old_opposing/enemy_total, and inactive commitment is fixed+mobile×old_opposing/(enemy_total+selected_opponent_term). Old commitments and resources must be finite nonnegative. Therefore enemy_total>0 guarantees every inactive denominator is positive, including the `first_inactive_opponent` alternative. A zero/nonfinite denominator in that branch indicates an invariant violation and invalidates the attempted period; it is never neutralized by the probability-ratio switch. If there is no inactive eligible front, the first-inactive term is not evaluated. All commitments are computed from old buffers. The asymmetric worked example never replaces this primary formula.

Threat balance uses neighbor incoming commitment /own outgoing commitment. Threat requires strictly greater than `min_threat`; exact equality does not qualify. Compare finite ratios using the deterministic arithmetic result with no epsilon/tolerance and no rounding before comparison. Democracies skip democratic candidates before choosing a maximum. Ties mean exact equality of the resulting finite ratios or equality of the analytic positive-infinity tags, and follow the selected tie switch. `equal_zero_neutral` treats zero/zero as ratio 1 even when minimum threat is altered. At own/opponent balance exactly equal to attack or victory threshold, either probability formula returns .5; this is a probabilistic test, not a strict threshold gate. The probability zero limits are printed: zero/positive→1, positive/zero→0; prose: zero/positive→0, positive/zero→1. Equal-zero override remains .5 for both directions.

## One complete period

Operate on a private candidate copy of engine state and RNG. A period succeeds only after invariants and finite science metrics validate; commit state, RNG and completed-period counter together. On error keep the prior committed model state and RNG, record attempted period/phase/error plus optional finite diagnostic fragment, mark the world invalid, and stop. Never report the failed attempt as completed or retry it automatically. This atomic rule prevents partial structural mutation from masquerading as a completed history.

1. **Allocation:** compute all commitments from last committed resources/actions/commitments. No owner change or current action is visible.
2. **Alignments:** form/reassess alliances from current allocated commitments and regimes. Determine prior-action obligations under the default; if the current-plans alternative is selected, defer its one-pass obligation derivation until baseline decisions exist.
3. **Decisions:** visit sovereign IDs in sorted order; setD if either previous action isD, otherwiseC. Add observed obligations. Only actors with noD may uniformly select one eligible sovereign neighbor, test attack probability, and if successful select a uniformly random victim border cell followed by uniformly random adjacent own agent cell. For a proposed attack from i to j, balance is i's commitment toward j divided by j's commitment toward i. If j belongs to an alliance whose named threat is i, replace only that denominator with the alliance's pooled commitments toward i. Collective-security deterrence is never added to this test. Record initiation flags. For `current_plans_once`, baseline initiation occurs before deriving new obligations; added obligations never undo a contemplated attack.
4. **Paths and interaction:** visit fronts by sorted key. If a unilateral or obligation action starts an unpathed battle, sample a path from theD actor using the same target-first rule. Two simultaneous proposals choose the lower-ID proposer path; this is the fixed `lower_id_path_priority` reconstruction, shown in inspection. A continuing battle retains its valid path; invalid paths are resampled from the lower-ID acting side. For mutualD, evaluate each side's own/opponent balance with the same configured victory threshold (default 2.2), without2003 reciprocal defender thresholds or geographic attack projection. Independent claims use two draws, first lower-ID side; `single_draw` uses one. If neither succeeds, one stalemate draw. A stalemate or any victory claim clears both current actions and the path after recording claims. Blocked/stale structural claims still terminate that battle. UnilateralD retains path/action to next period and has no combat consequence.
5. **Resource update:** independently replace each pre-conquest sovereign actor's resources with `10 + 10*tax_rate*sum(distance_gradient^distance(capital, province))` over its noncapital cells. Never tax capital twice. No damage is assessed. This phase uses ownership preceding claims.
6. **Structure:** shuffle the pre-conquest sovereign-ID list once if claims exist, process its claimant actors in that order, and each actor's claims by front key. Require winner/loser alive, agent/target still owned by those IDs and adjacent. Otherwise record stale claim and skip. Before applying a claim, determine its entire prospective ownership/release footprint. Under `affected_cells`, reject if any footprint cell or path agent is already locked, then lock that whole footprint and agent on success. Under `affected_states`, reject if winner, loser or any state retired by the claim is locked, then lock those affected identities and new identities on success. Unit victim transfers to winner; ordinary province transfers and unreachable cells release; compound-capital treatment follows the switch. Rebuild membership/front topology once after all claims. New states receive resources 10, not an immediate province extraction; surviving actors keep the phase5 resources, producing the explicit next-update lag. Fresh fronts startC/0. Surviving fronts retain directional buffers except any ended battle's actions/path. Retire invalid alliances/pariahs for the next period.
7. **Census:** validate ownership, contiguity, capital membership, regimes, IDs, fronts and alliance membership; compute finite statistics; write structural/battle counts; swap actions/commitments into previous buffers; commit candidate. Record first democratic extinction and first all-democratic occupation if newly reached.

Newly formed fronts do not inherit vanished states' combat memory. A captured cell follows winner governing regime while occupied regardless of latent-tag mode. Release creates one unitary state per disconnected cell, not a multi-cell splinter federation. Allies act only on existing territorial fronts; no long-distance combat or march-through rights exist. AllD-to-D stochastic combat remains possible if a custom prior obligation creates it, but primary tagging/alliances/security cannot create an initiating democratic–democratic attack; this invariant is verified rather than used as a reproduction judge.

Run the entire configured horizon even after democratic extinction, all-democratic occupation, hegemony or quiet periods. No absorbing-state shortcut consumes fewer scheduled clock iterations or changes result completeness. Grouped display ticks execute up to `periods_per_tick` full atomic periods, with a final partial display tick. Invalid/finished worlds do not advance. Export display tick, attempted/completed source periods, periods in last tick, horizon and finish reason. Model fingerprints and Outcome exclude display-only history lengths; grouped and ungrouped final science state must match.

## Outcomes, metrics and recording

Outcome is `complete` at the horizon or `invalid` with phase/context. Invalid outcomes retain prior committed state and partial observation series, but final metrics are unavailable for source inference. Setup errors are distinct construction failures and retained as attempted keys. Each registered history has exactly one result, never a substitute seed.

Every complete history reports final democratic cells/total cells, surviving sovereign counts by regime, mean/maximum state size by regime, number of conflict fronts, current allies/pariahs, democratic extinction indicator/time and all-democratic occupation indicator/time. First-passage times are null when not observed by horizon, not zero. Export integer cell counts as authoritative; fractions are derived. Mean/maximum size for a regime with no surviving states is null with reason `no_surviving_states`, a valid defined-availability status rather than a nonfinite arithmetic failure.

For a surviving democratic state with no external neighbor, exposure=1: the lattice is wholly its own democratic territory. Size-weighted exposure averages neighbor fractions with weights equal to democratic states' cell counts; clustering ratio divides that average by the configured initial share, not realized setup share. At configured share0 the ratio is `undefined_initial_density`; at final democratic extinction it is `undefined_extinction`. Both are valid complete-history statuses. Never impute zero or drop the history from territorial-share or extinction inference. Equal-state and border-edge switches alter only the explicitly named exposure/weighting steps.

A complete population includes all registered results even when individual clustering is legitimately undefined. Clustering summaries condition on democratic survivors and report numerator count, complete-history count and extinction count. No survivors makes a population clustering summary unavailable; invalid histories prevent registered inference rather than reduce its attempted n.

Battle census records initiated fronts, mutualD front-periods, completed battles by victory/stalemate, independent opposing claims and successful/stale/locked structural claims. These are exact engine counters, not casualties or2003 war records. UI event logs are bounded and separately report dropped events; they never replace the full counters or change RNG. Registered output keeps setup counts, resolved config, final full state hash, outcome status and science vector; optional deterministic traces are for probes, not required to retain every period of every history.
