### Rules and limits of the source checks

The 2002 paper defines y from agents that "became extremists" without giving a numerical
threshold. For drawn extremist opinions we name the assumption: past the innermost initial
extremist minus 0.1. For AD's extremists at ±1, counting past 0.9 is also an assumption.
The reply's explicit 0.8 → 0.7 change applies to M&C's band initialization. Neither a count
past a position nor y alone shows that an agent's uncertainty decreased. Physical population
drift is reported alongside the position-based indicator.

The reply acknowledges that the horizon and cutoff were not specified in 2002. It lowers
the cutoff after inspecting the trajectories. The episode says so. Its comparison follows
M&C's Java program, which updates both agents from their old states; the published Python
appendix instead updates one listener per meeting and uses a different horizon.

The Fig. 5 recruitment-occurrence rule, Fig. 7–8 example-occurrence rule, DNAW broad visual
tolerance, AD transition-onset rule, sparse-network cutoff comparison and direct reply
large-population rule **were revised after the initial result was known**. They replace
tests that imposed typical frequencies or unstated thresholds on the source. Their new
thresholds are named operational checks, not measurements specified by the authors.
All rules and verdicts remain visible in `survey/src/claims/agreement.rs`.

Zero observations in 1,000 independently sampled runs give a one-sided 95 % upper bound of
about 0.299 % on the measured event's frequency, conditional on this implementation and
counting convention. They do not establish mathematical impossibility. The interaction
strength μ = 0.5 is repeated in the 2002 text and later figures; μ = 0.2 producing the
displayed patterns does not establish a misprinted caption. Its trajectory clocks do not
explicitly normalize individual meetings, so the reconstructions label our N-meeting periods.

AD's moderate opinions are uniform across the full [−1, 1] interval, independent of extremists
at ±1. Uniform-edge sampling is our named convention; the paper leaves pair sampling open.
Its sparse-ring clusters can lie between 0.7 and 0.9. Changing a cutoff changes their labels,
not their opinions. Neither the capped network runs nor finite N measurements establish an
infinite-population limit. With an odd rounded extremist total, configured δ = 0 has
a randomly chosen one-agent surplus; for example, N 1,000 and pe 0.025 gives
realized |δ| = 0.04. This limits quantitative readings of odd-count Fig. 9 cells.
All explicit Fig. 5/7 and pe 0.05/0.1 population probes use even extremist totals.

## How to film it

Reuse episode 5's felt histogram, with the ruler labeled −1 to +1. Each agent stands in its
opinion bin and wears its starting opinion's yarn color (`start`); each camp reads as a crowd.
The new `agreement` shot records each agent's opinion **and uncertainty** at every kept period,
along with its initial role and starting opinion. True simulation periods remain available
when a shot samples every fifth or twentieth period.

Uncertainty has two visible encodings. On the felt, a cream marker above each agent is short
when confident and longer when uncertain; its length changes with that agent's measured
uncertainty. The opening close-up draws the actual opinion segments for a selected pair on
a ruler, labels their uncertainties, and shows how overlap permits asymmetric influence.
The opinion × time panel extends `blender/overlays/opinions.py`, colored by current uncertainty
with a fixed red-to-green legend, while the bodies retain the starting-opinion colors. The
legend distinguishes initially extreme roles from present confidence: they are not identical.

The replication comparison uses the same seed and band setup, with identical trajectories
up to the first stop. A counting line visibly moves from ±0.8 to ±0.7; the lower-cutoff beat
labels the authors' later measurement choice. Its indicator panel shows all four combinations,
not just one favorable seed. Population and imbalance comparisons show measured frequencies
beside representative runs. A lattice beat uses the existing lattice display or a faithful
histogram plus the opinion-time panel; no decorative network implies unrecorded interactions.
