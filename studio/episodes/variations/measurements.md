## Captions

- holds: Schelling's checkerboard has been rebuilt many times; each rebuild changes one of his rules — each variation differs from s71-board in its movers, movement, start, edges or neighborhood: pv-flat: movers anyone, movement best; gvn-segregated: movers anyone, movement random; svw-small: movement random, start deleted_checkerboard, edges torus; zhang-checkerboard: movement swap, start checkerboard, edges torus.
- holds: Pancs and Vriend: anyone may move, any time, to the square they like best — pv-flat moves anyone, to the best square.
- holds: with Schelling's wishes, most runs end split in two — 17 of 20 end in two clusters (their count; mean 2.15; theirs 91 %, 2.10).
- holds: wanting a mixed street, up to half and half, splits them even more often — p50: 19 of 20 in two clusters against 17 (theirs 98 % and 91 %).
- holds: in a ring, even Flumps who like half and half best end in two groups — 20 of 20 in two groups at 100,000 turns (p100).
- holds: Gauvin and colleagues: anyone may move to a square that suits; tolerance works like temperature — gvn movers anyone, movement random.
- holds: tolerate too little, and nobody can move — at T = 0.3, 20 of 20 end with nobody moving; median s 0.025.
- holds: tolerate up to half, and two great clusters form — at T = 1/2, median s 1.000, median clusters 2.
- holds: tolerate most, and the town stays mixed — at T = 0.8, median s 0.053, median clusters 292.
- holds: Singh and colleagues: in a small town, the same wishes make two clusters — 8 × 8: 20 of 20 end in two clusters (joined at sides or corners).
- holds: in a city of 100 by 100, dozens; Schelling's striking picture is a small-town effect — 100 × 100: median 58 clusters (theirs 55).
- holds: Zhang: no empty houses; neighbors trade homes, and everyone likes half and half best — 10000 Flumps on 10000 squares, swap, tent utility (best at half alike).
- holds: from a perfect mix, they sort anyway — mixed pairs 20000 at the start, 3620 at step 100, 1971 at step 1,000 (medians).
- holds: two claims don't reproduce here: Zhang's waiting times, and that wanting only a perfect mix acts like wanting half and half — spiked 6.85 clusters against p100's 4.25 (means; their footnote 23: "very similar"); Zhang: 8,000 mixed pairs by step 10 (median), 100,000 draws, against his 40 million.
- holds: change the rules, and the town still sorts, unless people tolerate a lot, or can't move at all — s 0.02 frozen, 1.00 at half, 0.05 at 0.8; Pancs & Vriend's flat 17 of 20 split.
